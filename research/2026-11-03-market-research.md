# INA Lamp: market research and strategic options

Research date: 3 November 2026. Status: decision brief, not an approved business plan.

## Recommendation

Build toward a small crochet design and learning business, with lampshades as the signature product. Use real finished pieces to develop original designs, demonstrate results, create content, and take occasional profitable premium orders. Develop tested patterns as the first repeatable offer; introduce supported learning or kits only when buyers demonstrate a need.

Start with one original design and one main sales channel. Etsy is the strongest initial candidate for patterns and a limited physical offering. Treat Whatnot as an optional finished-stock experiment. Delay a full Shopify shop until independent traffic and sales justify maintaining it.

This recommendation reflects the founder's current stage: London-based, practising a first shade, with estimated production time of 8–10 hours. It is not evidence that patterns will reliably provide a full income. Making, designing, writing instructions, and teaching are separate skills that need validation.

## What the evidence supports

There is an established category, with retail crochet shades, specialist makers, paid patterns and free tutorials. There is also meaningful price competition. We did not obtain reliable niche sales volume, UK keyword demand, competitor profit, or product-specific conversion rates. Public prices establish alternatives available to customers, not willingness to pay for INA.

Etsy's spring/summer report describes interest in handcrafted textures and personal interiors. Its crochet sales growth relates to clothing, not lampshades, and its underlying data is a US marketplace snapshot through February 2026. It supports testing the aesthetic, not projecting UK lampshade growth. [Etsy trend report](https://www.etsy.com/ca/seller-handbook/article/1473931456647).

The Etsy category page displayed 696+ items, mixing downloads and physical goods. This is neither a count of unique competitors nor a measure of demand. Ratings beside results cannot safely be interpreted as sales for those individual products. [Etsy category snapshot](https://www.etsy.com/market/lamp_shade_crochet).

## The opportunity to test

Proposed positioning: **Create a distinctive, warm corner of your home—with a design you can confidently make yourself.**

The potential gap is a cohesive buying and making experience around a recognisable design: beautiful finished results, obtainable UK materials, clear fit information, and reliable instructions. Competitors already supply parts of this experience. INA must prove that its particular combination is worth choosing and paying for.

Priority customer hypothesis: an existing crocheter who can follow a pattern, wants an interesting home project, and is willing to buy instructions that reduce sourcing and fitting uncertainty. Avoid starting with complete beginners on a lengthy lampshade project: teaching every basic stitch could overwhelm support capacity.

Secondary customer: someone who wants the finished design and will pay a premium for Ivana to make it. Keep these offers and calls to action distinct. People who enjoy watching crochet are not necessarily pattern buyers or premium décor buyers.

Test three potential advantages:

1. **Recognisable design.** One memorable silhouette or colour language, photographed both lit and unlit in a real room. Reject a merely generic boho look if people cannot distinguish it from cheaper alternatives.
2. **Confidence of completion.** Exact frame dimensions, gauge, UK/US stitch terminology, materials list, assembly photos, troubleshooting, and a small number of genuinely supported sizes. Independent makers must reproduce the result.
3. **Convenient sourcing.** A verified UK shopping list with available substitutes. Start with links and specifications; only hold kit inventory if customers actually struggle to source parts and will pay for the convenience.

Upcycled frames may be attractive creatively, but irregular sizes make scalable instructions and compatibility harder. Begin with a repeatable frame specification. Do not claim sustainability without evidence for the particular materials and process.

## Three possible approaches

| Direction | Main offer | Strength | Main weakness | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Original patterns with limited finished pieces | Patterns, then bundles or focused video support | Reuses design work without remaking each sale | Needs design skill, traffic, testing and support | Recommended first direction |
| B. Guided projects and later kits | Small-group crochet-alongs; subsequently materials kits | Higher potential order value; addresses confidence and sourcing | Teaching time, inventory and fulfilment; beginner outcome risk | Expansion after A works |
| C. Premium bespoke textile lighting | High-priced commissions; later designer collaborations | Fewer customers needed for meaningful revenue | 8–10-hour production, service time, safety and premium-demand uncertainty | Limited side offer initially |

Do not launch all three together. Do not add unrelated crochet products merely to fill a catalogue. If lampshade demand proves too narrow, test a faster adjacent home project using the same design language before abandoning the identity.

## Channel choices

**Etsy first:** one shop can offer original downloads and clearly separated finished items. Search intent is useful to test, but discovery is not guaranteed. Mark every download clearly as a pattern, not a finished lamp. The pattern must be Ivana's own original work; practising someone else's pattern does not create an original pattern to resell. [Etsy creativity standards](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity/).

**Whatnot as a later experiment:** the current seller policy requires dispatch within two business days. Use ready-to-ship pieces and fixed prices or opening bids above the calculated minimum. Do not build the initial business around taking long commissions live. The UK fee table shows starting commission of 6.67% plus VAT and payment processing of 2.42% + £0.25 plus VAT; bases differ, with processing including shipping and taxes. [Shipping policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/43295059160973-Shipping-Policy-for-Sellers), [country-specific fees](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/4847069165965-Whatnot-seller-fees). No niche Whatnot sales data was verified, so audience fit is still unknown.

**Shopify later:** UK Basic currently lists £25/month paid monthly or £19/month billed yearly, with card rates from 2% + £0.25. It is a checkout and store, not evidence of demand. [Shopify UK pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing). At an illustrative £9 pattern price, the model above implies roughly £1.18/order fee savings against Etsy before Shopify tax, apps or other differences; about 22 self-generated orders would cover £25. That arithmetic does not justify moving Etsy-discovered demand or losing marketplace acquisition. Revisit when independently generated sales are repeatable and maintenance time is affordable.

**Content:** choose the platform Ivana is most comfortable sustaining, provisionally Instagram. Reuse strong still images for a small Pinterest test if capacity permits. These are proposed experiments, not verified claims of superior conversion. Do not rely on creator advertising revenue or sponsorships in the initial income model.

## Efficient use of making time

Capture short clips at planned milestones: materials and frame, signature stitch, assembly, and the finished room transformation. Avoid recording and editing the entire nine-hour process. From one design, produce a room-reveal clip, a process clip, one useful explanation, and product photography. Put a time cap on editing and record it as work.

Use different calls to action: “make this design” for patterns, “buy this finished piece” for décor buyers. Show the finished result early; use the making story to establish authenticity and explain value. Until she is ready to teach, describe the content honestly as learning and development.

Keep one record per design: version, measurements, materials, cost, timed production, tester feedback, content, and customer questions. AI can help organise notes and draft copy, but Ivana and independent testers must verify stitch counts and instructions against real makes.

## Product constraints that affect the business

Selling a shade separately reduces the initial scope compared with assembling an electrical lamp, but does not remove product-safety duties. GB guidance requires products to be safe under normal and foreseeable use and appropriately supported with information. Northern Ireland follows a different general product-safety regime. [OPSS guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-product-safety-regulations-2005/general-product-safety-regulations-2005-great-britain).

Before commercial release, get product-specific advice from Trading Standards or a suitable lighting test specialist about the exact construction, frame, heat exposure, materials, clearance, fit and instructions. Do not invent a universal safe wattage or assume an “LED only” label proves safety. Confirm evidence and any applicable testing before setting bulb limits or making safety claims. Apply the resulting design constraints to pattern instructions too.

A documented, repeatable shade-only design is the proposed starting scope. Avoid arbitrary customer-selected electrical configurations. Factor suitable insurance, packaging, damage and returns into actual costing. Returns terms must reflect UK consumer rights; do not assume that simply making a standard item after ordering eliminates cancellation rights. [Government returns guidance](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds).

## Evidence needed before committing to a business plan

The following are proposed evaluation gates for approval, not a launch schedule or authorised external actions:

1. **Demand specificity:** use Etsy Marketplace Insights to compare “crochet lampshade pattern”, “crochet lamp shade”, “lampshade crochet kit”, “scalloped lampshade”, “colourful lampshade”, and related terms. Record date, search volume, competing listings and intent; do not equate searches with purchases. Etsy offers 15 free searches/week and last-30-day search/listing data. These account-level figures have not been accessed. [Marketplace Insights](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/35122361353239-How-Do-I-Use-Etsy-s-Marketplace-Insights-Tool).
2. **Repeatability:** finish an original design and repeat it, logging actual time, material use and errors. Do not assume the early 8–10-hour estimate is mature production speed.
3. **Independent usability:** three independent crocheters complete the pattern, including at least two at the intended skill level. Resolve all blocking errors before paid release. Record completion time and every support request.
4. **Customer evidence:** discuss specific designs and actual prices with about ten relevant potential buyers, separated into makers and finished-product buyers. These interviews have not happened. Compliments and poll votes are weaker evidence than purchases.
5. **Commercial pilot:** proposed initial gate of ten full-price pattern purchases by people outside friends/family, with measured acquisition and support time. This would justify further testing, not a full-income claim. If 200 relevant product-page visits produce fewer than two purchases, inspect price, clarity, sourcing friction and audience before adding products. These thresholds are internal decision rules, not industry benchmarks.
6. **Physical feasibility:** only test finished-product sales after product safety is addressed and the quoted price covers all measured labour. If premium demand is absent, retain shades as development assets rather than subsidising customer orders.

Success metric: contribution after materials, fees, acquisition and support, divided by all founder hours. Track product-page visits, purchases, refunds, completion outcomes, and source of customers alongside it. Followers and views alone do not establish a business.

## Decision requested

Approve or revise **A: original patterns as the initial scalable offer, supported by real finished pieces and focused content; B as a later expansion; C as a tightly limited premium option.**

Before producing the business plan, establish launch budget, weekly working hours, desired monthly income, current audience, and whether Ivana is already designing independently or learning from existing patterns. The business plan should then set specific responsibilities, budgets, milestones and stop/go decisions. No shops, outreach, purchases or public listings have been created during this research.
