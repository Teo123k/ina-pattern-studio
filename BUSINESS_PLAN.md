# INA Lamp — business and execution plan

Prepared 3 November 2026. Planning horizon: 12 weeks from the actual start date, followed by evidence-led expansion. All prices, budgets and thresholds below are planning hypotheses unless explicitly identified as observed facts.

## 1. The business we are building

**INA helps people who already crochet create distinctive lighting for their homes, using original designs, clear instructions and easy-to-source materials.**

Ivana develops the designs and demonstrates them through real finished pieces. The initial repeatable product is a tested digital pattern. Finished shades are occasional premium orders and visual proof of the work. Guided learning and materials kits are later offers, introduced only when customers demonstrate demand.

The aim of the first 12 weeks is to establish whether one original design can attract paying customers and deliver a successful making experience. It is not to replace an income immediately.

Confirmed: London base; Ivana is practising her first shade; estimated production time is 8–10 hours. Unknown: budget, available hours, original-design experience, audience and desired income. For scheduling, assume **15 Ivana hours and 3 business-support hours per week**, with no existing audience. These are capacity assumptions, not commitments. If availability is lower, extend the timeline rather than reducing testing.

## 2. The market position

The opportunity is a better-supported, recognisable crochet home project. It is not an empty market: research found £32–£34 retail crochet shades, a £3 designer pattern, and a US$3 pattern with a free version. Price competition and existing tutorials mean INA must earn a premium through design quality and ease of completion. See the [dated competitor research](research/2026-11-03-market-research.md).

**Primary customer:** a UK-based crocheter who can follow written patterns, wants a distinctive home project, and needs confidence about frame choice, materials and assembly.

**Secondary customer:** a décor buyer who wants the finished INA design and will pay for Ivana's time. These customers receive a separate offer and call to action.

The proposed gap is a hypothesis: buyers value a distinctive result plus clear UK sourcing and fitting enough to pay above bargain-pattern prices. Validate it with interviews, independent makes and full-price purchases. Views and compliments do not establish demand.

Positioning line: **“A statement shade, made by you.”** Supporting message: “Original crochet designs by Ivana, with clear instructions from first stitch to final fitting.” Use only after the product actually meets that promise.

Choose one visual signature through customer testing: a recognisable edge, silhouette, or colour arrangement. Start with one repeatable frame size and two photographed colourways. Do not promise universal fit or untested sizes. INA Lamp remains a working name until availability checks are completed before public investment.

## 3. The first product

Working name: **INA 01 — Signature Lampshade Pattern**. Final style and dimensions are selected after prototype and customer feedback.

Initial price test: **£9**, with no introductory discount during the first ten-customer test. This is a hypothesis above several observed competitors, not a proven market price. Record the actual customer-facing checkout price, including any buyer tax added by the platform.

Included:

- Original written pattern, edited for clarity, with stitch counts and gauge.
- One tested size, exact frame dimensions, fitting specification, yarn quantity and hook size.
- UK materials guide with two checked sourcing options where available; never substitute materials in ways that invalidate assessed performance.
- Assembly photographs, three short videos for the difficult steps, and troubleshooting.
- Clearly stated skill level, estimated time based on testers, and UK/US terminology explained consistently.
- Care and use instructions appropriate to the assessed design, a version number and a corrections route.

The listing must say **DIGITAL PATTERN — NO FINISHED LAMPSHADE OR MATERIALS INCLUDED** prominently. Show the real finished product, frame detail, scale, materials, and a sample instruction page. Explain that the lamp base/electrical components are not supplied.

Release criteria: Ivana reproduces the design; three independent testers complete it, including two at the intended skill level; no unresolved blocking errors remain; sourcing and fitting instructions are checked; the lighting design and instructions have received appropriate product-specific safety review. A successful crochet test does not establish heat or electrical safety.

If Ivana is practising from an existing pattern, treat it as learning. Do not repackage it for sale. Etsy requires digital designs to be the seller's own original work. [Etsy creativity standards](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity/).

## 4. Revenue model and expansion order

| Stage | Offer | Pricing approach | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | INA 01 pattern | Test £9 | Product release criteria passed |
| Next | Second original design; two-pattern bundle | Test £9 each / £16 bundle | First design reaches 25 paid sales, support is manageable and buyers request more |
| Later | Guided online crochet-along | Test £35/person, pattern included; materials separate | At least six interested customers and adequate teaching skill |
| Optional | Materials kit | Cost only after sourcing and packing tests | At least ten relevant enquiries specifically about buying materials together |
| Limited | Finished shade | Quote from measured total costs and labour | Safety and shipping ready; buyer accepts profitable price |

Interest triggers preparation, not proof of sales. For a guided pilot, set a minimum of six paid seats and explicit cancellation/refund terms if it does not run. Run two 90-minute teaching sessions with independent making between sessions; do not promise a complete eight-hour project in one class. Cap at eight people. Allow seven total host hours including preparation, support and administration.

Keep physical commissions to one per month initially, and only if that fits capacity. Each order must have an agreed specification and delivery date. Do not add unlimited custom sizing.

Kits and workshops are options, not mandatory milestones. Skip them if demand or margins are weak. Exclude sponsorships and platform advertising revenue from the base financial case.

## 5. Financial rules

Use actual receipts and platform statements as soon as available. The model below is a planning illustration for a UK seller who does not reclaim fee VAT, with no paid acquisition.

Etsy's published UK charges include 6.5% transaction fees, 4% + £0.20 processing, a 0.48% regulatory fee and a US$0.20 listing fee converted to GBP. Model the listing at £0.15. Advertising and applicable taxes can add costs. [Etsy UK fees](https://www.etsy.com/uk/sell).

Modelled fee = (price × 10.98% + £0.35) × 1.20. The VAT uplift is a conservative modelling assumption; replace it with the actual tax treatment. Check Offsite Ads settings and charges before release; the base model excludes them.

At £9, modelled fees are £1.61. Allow five minutes of support at £15/hour (£1.25), leaving **£6.14 contribution per pattern** before design development, content, overhead, advertising and tax.

| Monthly pattern sales | Revenue | Contribution after modelled fees and support |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10 | £90 | £61 |
| 50 | £450 | £307 |
| 100 | £900 | £614 |
| 250 | £2,250 | £1,535 |

These are scenarios, not forecasts. If ten monthly content/admin hours are valued at £15 and cash overhead is £25, deduct another £175. The 50-sale scenario then leaves about £132 before development recovery and tax. The model improves only if sales grow without equivalent growth in work.

An illustrative 40-hour pattern development investment at £15/hour is £600 of labour. Recovering it requires about 98 sales at £6.14 contribution, before other setup costs. Record the actual hours; early learning may take longer. Founder labour is an economic cost even when it is not a cash payment.

For a guided pilot: six people at £35 produces £210. Reserving 15% for checkout/refunds and £105 for seven host hours leaves £73.50 before tools, marketing, tax and other costs. At eight participants the equivalent remainder is £133. These are rough feasibility figures, not verified platform fees or a forecast. Recalculate when choosing checkout.

For physical products, use:

`Minimum price = (materials + packaging + delivery subsidy + risk allowance + all labour + overhead allocation + desired surplus + fixed fee) / (1 - percentage fee)`

Example: £25 materials + £6 packaging + £5 risk + 11 total hours × £15 + £10 overhead + £30 surplus, using 13.176% variable fees and £0.42 fixed, gives about **£278** before buyer-paid shipping and its fees. Nine making hours plus two admin/packing hours is an assumption. This is a cost floor to test, not evidence customers will pay £278. If buyers reject a profitable price, reduce physical production rather than underpaying Ivana.

## 6. Spending plan

Proposed discovery/prototype cash ceiling: **£300**, released in stages. This is not a complete commercial-launch budget and does not authorise purchases.

| Use | Maximum planning allocation |
|---|---:|
| Prototype frames, yarn and small tools | £90 |
| Three tester material reimbursements | £90 |
| Basic photo/phone support equipment, only if needed | £20 |
| Shop setup/listings/domain allowance | £30 |
| Contingency | £70 |
| Total | £300 |

Spend at most £90 before the first customer/design review. Reimburse tester materials rather than treating this allocation as payment for their labour; agree arrangements clearly and do not require positive public reviews. Use existing equipment and free tools initially. Actual shop setup fees may differ; reallocate within the ceiling or revisit it.

**Unquoted separate costs:** safety advice/testing, relevant insurance, professional tax/legal help if needed, and physical shipping trials. Obtain quotes after documenting the design, before commercial commitments. Total launch cash = prototype spending + these confirmed costs. If unaffordable, continue research and unlit development while reassessing the product; do not assume a PDF bypasses the design-safety issue.

No bulk inventory, paid advertising, Shopify subscription or substantial branding spend in the initial validation budget.

## 7. Sales and content system

Start with **Etsy for transactions and Instagram for the maker story and project demonstration**. Instagram is a working choice; use Ivana's existing strongest channel if she has one. Do not open multiple active content channels at once.

Customer path: finished-room reveal → detail showing how the project is made → clear skill/material information → Etsy pattern purchase → successful make → optional feedback and next design.

Publish two short posts and one photo/carousel weekly, within a three-hour weekly content allowance. Capture clips during normal work; batch editing once a week. Alternate the finished result, one helpful fitting/stitch lesson, and an honest development story. Before release, invite people to follow the project; after release, link directly to the pattern. Do not imply the pattern is ready while testers are still finding errors.

Use permission-based craft communities to find testers and interviewees, observing group rules. The plan describes founder actions; no outreach has been sent. Do not add Etsy buyers to a marketing list without appropriate consent.

Once the shop is available, use Etsy Marketplace Insights for relevant search terms and compare actual buyer intent. Avoid titles that attract finished-lamp buyers to a PDF. Keep the design and price constant while testing a new first photograph or title, and record the change. Small samples will be directional rather than statistically conclusive.

Review Shopify only after independently generated purchases repeat for two months and the expected contribution covers its subscription, tools and maintenance. Whatnot comes after there is finished stock and live-selling demand; its two-business-day dispatch policy makes it unsuitable as the initial long-commission workflow. [Whatnot shipping policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/43295059160973-Shipping-Policy-for-Sellers).

## 8. Twelve-week execution sequence

Weeks are capacity estimates, not deadlines that override product readiness. The full budgeted capacity is 180 Ivana hours plus 36 support hours. Record all of it, including development and marketing.

| When | Main work | Owner | Required output / decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Finish practice shade; log every task and cost; clarify whether design is original; document frame/materials | Ivana | Baseline product record; initial specification for safety advice |
| Week 2 | Interview 10 relevant people, primarily existing crocheters; compare 3 simple design directions; request safety advice/quotes | Business support + Ivana | Written problems, sourcing concerns and reactions to £9; choose one direction or revise |
| Weeks 3–4 | Develop one original design and repeat it; photograph steps; write draft pattern; obtain sourcing options | Ivana | Two consistent examples and a complete draft; safety requirements understood |
| Weeks 5–6 | Three independent tester makes; record every question; fix counts, fit and instructions | Ivana + testers | Three completed makes; no blocking errors; revised instructions |
| Week 7 | Complete required safety work; prepare listing, videos, policies and actual cost model | Ivana + business support | Release checklist complete; otherwise extend this phase |
| Week 8 | Publish one tested offer; launch focused content; support customers | Ivana + business support | First paid orders if demand exists; measurement starts |
| Weeks 9–10 | Improve one bottleneck at a time; track visits, purchases, support, completion and refunds | Business support | Seek 10 full-price non-friend/family customers; review customer outcomes |
| Weeks 11–12 | Evaluate contribution and founder hours; interview buyers; decide next investment | Both | Continue, revise or stop decision recorded; second product only if its trigger is met |

“Business support” is a proposed role for Teo or another named helper, not an assumed commitment. If Ivana works alone, reserve roughly three of her weekly hours for these tasks and extend development. AI can draft documents and organise evidence; it does not replace customer conversations, original design, testing or qualified product advice.

## 9. Operating responsibilities

Ivana owns design, materials, physical tests, instruction accuracy, content authenticity and customer technical support. Business support owns costing, records, customer interviews, listing administration and the weekly review. Both decide spending and product expansion.

Weekly rhythm before launch: 8 hours design/making, 3 hours instructions/testing, 3 hours content, 1 hour review for Ivana. After launch shift development time toward support and proven demand. Respond to routine customer questions within two business days; log actual support time. Publish corrections promptly and provide updated instructions to affected buyers through permitted platform tools.

Store each product's measurements, material receipts, timed builds, pattern versions, assessment evidence and tester notes together. Keep one current pattern version. Maintain financial records from the start and check the appropriate sole-trader registration responsibilities rather than assuming a limited company is necessary. [UK sole-trader guidance](https://www.gov.uk/become-sole-trader).

Before selling, confirm required seller information, digital delivery/cancellation handling, and applicable tax treatment for the chosen markets. Do not use a blanket “no refunds” statement. For initial physical orders, limit geography to Great Britain while requirements for other markets are assessed. Pattern instructions should clearly identify the supported sourcing/fitting region; review international digital-selling requirements separately. [Online-selling rules](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/online-selling).

For the exact lampshade construction, obtain appropriate advice on material behaviour, bulb clearance, heat, fitting and foreseeable use. Do not invent a safe wattage or describe an unassessed product as certified. Include actual assessment and instructions in the release evidence. [UK product-safety guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/product-safety-advice-for-businesses).

## 10. How we decide whether it works

Use [EXECUTION.md](EXECUTION.md) for task status and weekly actuals. The following are internal decision rules, not market benchmarks.

| Signal | Decision |
|---|---|
| Testers cannot complete the project or there is a safety/design problem | Hold release; resolve and retest affected steps |
| Fewer than 200 relevant product-page visits after launch | Demand is inconclusive; improve acquisition before declaring the product rejected |
| 200 relevant visits with fewer than 2 purchases | Review audience, imagery, offer clarity and price; make one documented change |
| 10 full-price purchases outside friends/family, no unresolved quality issue | Continue testing; this is an early signal, not product-market fit |
| Support averages over 15 minutes/order | Fix instructions before adding customers; £9 economics may be too weak |
| 25 paid purchases, average support at or below 10 minutes, positive contribution and repeat interest | Permit development of one second design |
| Two documented offer tests, each with about 200 relevant visits, still produce fewer than 2 purchases each | Pause new lampshade development; reconsider segment or test a faster adjacent crochet home project |
| Continuing activity fails to pay an acceptable return on total time | Keep as a limited side project or change the model before scaling |

Ask buyers for honest completion feedback after enough time to make the project; non-response is unknown, not success. Any safety complaint is investigated immediately. Track refunds in both count and reason; do not overinterpret a percentage from ten orders.

Cash result = revenue − actual fees − refunds − materials/fulfilment − marketing − other cash costs. Economic result then deducts all unpaid founder time at the chosen hourly target. Do not subtract support labour twice when reconciling the contribution model with total founder hours.

The first 12 weeks may end with a validated product and little income, or with evidence to stop. Both are useful outcomes if spending and time remain controlled. Full-time potential requires repeatable acquisition, a useful catalogue, and an acceptable return across several months.

## 11. First actions

Start with the current shade, a timer and a materials record. Finish it, document what was hard, and identify what is original. Then test the design proposition with ten relevant people and establish the safety requirements before investing in a second commercial prototype. Confirm the assumed weekly capacity and spending ceiling during that first review.

Do not rush a Christmas launch from a November start; follow the readiness gates. The immediate objective is one product that customers can successfully make and will pay for.
